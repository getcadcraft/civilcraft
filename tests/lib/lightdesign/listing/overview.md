Light one room at a time. Give it an outline, a ceiling height, how reflective the surfaces are and the luminaires (a maker's .ies or .ldt file, or a generic type to try it), and it works out the lux on the workplane, the uniformity, glare (UGR) and watts per square metre, with the light that bounces off the floor, walls and ceiling included. Add windows and it gives a daylight factor under the standard overcast sky. List furniture and partitions and they block the direct light. Draw an escape route and it checks the emergency lighting on the floor.

It works in CadCraft (rooms from closed shapes, results drawn on layers) and in BimCraft (rooms and windows from the model, luminaires placed as light fixtures in the room's level, lux written into the plan view). The numbers are a design aid, not a certificate: the person responsible for the lighting design checks the result.
