It is a design aid, not a certificate, and nobody has compared a result with DIALux or another lighting program on the same room. Walls do not block light from each other, so a room that is not a box reads a little too bright in its bays; furniture you list blocks direct light only. Daylight has no sun, clear sky, ground reflection, buildings or trees outside, the same glass transmittance at every angle and no frames. The requirement numbers for rooms (EN 12464-1 style) and for emergency lighting (EN 1838 style) are typed from memory, unverified, and waiting for a lighting engineer; never read them as the standard's. Emergency lighting counts direct floor light only: no signs, duration or switch-over time. UGR is an estimate not compared with a published table. There is no catalogue of makers' luminaires in the add-on: a catalogue is the files you add, and its names, makers, lumens and watts are what each file says (the beam angle is worked out here). Road lighting is for a straight, level road and never a compliance check: check every result against your class table. It ships no road-surface reflection table (the CIE r-tables are in paid documents nobody here has), so luminance, its longitudinal uniformity, the threshold increment and the veiling luminance ratio are worked out only from a table you paste; the grid spacing rule of EN 13201-3 is not built in (you type the grid); the class values offered are EN 13201-2's M, C and P classes as reproduced in Sørensen's 2013 NMF textbook and the RP-8-00 illuminance table as reproduced by the FHWA in 2014, not read from the standards; the eye illuminance for glare is taken on a plane facing along the line of sight. No curves, junctions, wet roads, semi-cylindrical or hemispherical illuminance, HS, SC or EV classes, or energy indicators (EN 13201-5), and the luminaires are not drawn into CivilCraft. Exterior site lighting is the ground lux of CivilCraft's outdoor areas. The 3D lux surface is a picture of the grid, drawn in the panel (and as display bodies in ModelCraft), not a rendering of the room. PartCraft has no adapter: it models parts, with no rooms or floors to light. The BimCraft window height is assumed 1.2 m and the room's ceiling height is the walls' usual height. SuperCad Studio needs no adapter of its own: the add-on runs through the mounted apps' adapters.
