## How shortcuts are written

The help writes each shortcut the way your keyboard labels it.

| Written | On a Mac | On Windows and Linux |
|---|---|---|
| {{key:Mod+S}} | Command (⌘) and S | Ctrl and S |
| {{key:Ctrl+Shift+F}} | Control (⌃), Shift (⇧) and F | Ctrl, Shift and F |
| {{key:Alt+Left}} | Option (⌥) and ← | Alt and ← |
| {{key:Backspace}} | the Delete key | Backspace |
| {{key:Delete}} | fn and Delete (forward delete) | Delete |
| {{key:Enter}} | Return | Enter |

A plus sign means hold the keys together. "Then" means press one after the other.

Most app shortcuts accept either Command or Control on a Mac, so {{key:Mod+Z}} works both ways. A few, such as full screen ({{key:Ctrl+Shift+F}}), use the Control key on every computer; the help says so where that applies.

## Function keys on a Mac

On most Mac keyboards the top row changes brightness, volume and media. To send F1 to F12:

- hold **fn** (or the globe key) while you press the key, or
- open **System Settings**, then **Keyboard**, then **Keyboard Shortcuts**, then **Function Keys**, and turn on "Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys".

Some F-keys are also claimed by macOS itself (for example Mission Control). If a key does nothing in the app, try it with fn, or use the toolbar button instead.

## Shortcuts the browser keeps

A web page cannot take some shortcuts from the browser, whatever it does. In every app:

- {{key:Mod+N}}, {{key:Mod+T}} and {{key:Mod+W}} always open or close browser windows and tabs. The apps use buttons for New and Close instead.
- {{key:Mod+Q}} on a Mac and {{key:Alt+F4}} on Windows quit the browser. If you have unsaved work, the browser asks first ("Leave site?"); choose to stay, then save.
- The browser's own full screen ({{pick:mac=Control-Command-F, or the green button on the window|windows linux=F11}}) still works. The apps also have their own screen modes on {{key:Ctrl+Shift+F}}, which can hide the app's bars too.

## Mouse buttons on a trackpad

The apps use the right button and the middle button. Without a three-button mouse:

:::mac
- **Right-click:** click with two fingers. Turn on "Secondary click" in **System Settings**, then **Trackpad**, if two-finger click does nothing. Holding {{key:Ctrl}} while you click opens the browser's menu, but inside a drawing some apps read it as an ordinary click (with Control held), so prefer the two-finger click.
- **Middle-button drag (pan):** each app offers another way to pan, such as holding {{key:Space}} while you drag or dragging with the right button. The app's keyboard page says which.
- **Wheel zoom:** two-finger scroll on the trackpad zooms in most apps. Pinch also zooms where the app supports it.
:::

:::windows linux
- **Right-click:** click with two fingers, or click the lower-right corner of the touchpad. This depends on your touchpad settings.
- **Middle-button drag (pan):** each app offers another way to pan, such as holding {{key:Space}} while you drag or dragging with the right button. The app's keyboard page says which.
- **Wheel zoom:** two-finger scroll on the touchpad zooms in most apps.
:::

## Typing in a text box

While you type in a box (a name, a note, a search), the app's one-letter shortcuts are off, so typing "L" types an L. Press {{key:Esc}} or click on the drawing to give the keys back to the app.
