The cloud is cut into a tiled octree with additive levels of detail: each tile holds the points on its own grid, its children hold the rest, so drawing a tile and its ancestors draws every point exactly once. Positions are stored as integers on a millimetre grid, so a point read back is the point read in. Cleaning is a log of edits (crop, delete, class, noise, ground) applied to each tile when it is read; the source tiles are never rewritten.

Ground is found tile by tile (256 m tiles with an overlap) with a progressive morphological filter (after Zhang and others, 2003, our own code): the lowest point in each grid cell, an opening that grows its window while the slope allows, then every point is compared with the ground grid and called ground within a tolerance (0.15 m, or 0.08 m on the survey-grade switch). Vegetation and buildings are told apart geometrically, from height above the ground and how flat the neighbourhood of a point is; there is no machine learning. Stray points are found by counting neighbours, or by the distance to the nearest ones.

The ground surface is built by CivilCraft's own constrained Delaunay TIN (the same code the LAS import uses), from the ground points thinned to one per cell. A stockpile is the volume of the surface above a base inside the boundary; the base is the lowest boundary point, a plane, a smooth curved surface or a TIN through the boundary, or a level you type. Samples on the boundary that stand above a robust fit (a tree on the line) are left out of the base. Cut and fill between two flights is computed on a grid of cells (default 1 m).
