## What you need

A phone with CivilCraft open in its browser on the drawing, with the Land records add-on switched on. The phone must be allowed to give its location to the site, and the page must be open over https.

## Walk the land

1. In the **Land records** tab, open **Field walk** and press **Start a walk**. The walk screen fills the phone.
2. Go to the first corner and stand still. The top of the screen shows how close the phone thinks it is: good, fair or poor, in meters.
3. Press **Mark corner**. The phone buzzes, beeps and says the point's number, so you need not look.
4. Walk to the next corner and press again. The sketch shows the outline so far and the area, with how much it may be off.
5. For a point that is not a corner (a well, a gate), type a name in the box before you press. Clear the box for the next corner.
6. Press **Finish**. The walk is kept with the drawing.

A press is refused when the phone reports it is off by more than your limit. Wait a moment and press again. **Undo** removes the last point.

## Press the button from a watch, a headset or a clicker

Tick **Use a watch, headset or clicker as the button**. The page then plays silence under the title "Field walk", so the watch shows it as what is playing. On the watch, play, pause or next marks a corner and previous removes the last point. The same works with a headset's button.

A Bluetooth page-turner or presenter needs no setting: Page Down or Enter marks a corner, Page Up removes the last one.

No app is put on the watch. Whether a given watch passes its buttons to the phone's browser depends on the watch and the phone; if it does not, use the screen or a clicker.

## A route recorded elsewhere

A watch or a fitness app can often save a recorded walk as a GPX file. Press **Open a walk or GPX file**. Waypoints become corners. A recorded route is reduced to its corners: the setting says how far the route may stray from a straight side. A GPX file does not say how accurate its points are, so no accuracy is shown for it.

## Into the drawing

Choose a walk and press **Add to the drawing as points and a parcel**. Each point becomes a survey point, and the corners become a parcel with a record marked Walked.

If the drawing has a coordinate system, the walk is placed where it really is. If it has none, the walk is placed beside what is drawn, north up and at true size.

## How good is it

A phone is rarely closer than a few meters, and worse under trees and beside buildings. Standing still and averaging steadies a point but does not make a phone into a survey instrument. The area is shown with a "give or take" worked out from the accuracy the phone reported at each corner; treat it as a guide to the size of the doubt.

A walk is a sketch for planning, for a first look and for talking to a surveyor. It is not a boundary survey and must not be used as one.
