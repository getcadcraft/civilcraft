Road vehicles and the aircraft's nose gear use CivilCraft's own swept path solver: each unit turns about its rear axle, the front axle or the hitch pulls the next one along an exact tractrix, and reversing follows the last axle. An aircraft adds points (wing tips, tail, engines) fixed to the main gear's frame and carried through every step. A light rail car keeps both bogies on the track line and sets the body between them, so the middle sits inside the track and the ends swing out. A clearance check tests every body outline at every step against each obstacle line or parcel, and reports the first step that comes within the margin. A roundabout is circles and leg edges, with movements as smoothed paths through the lane. Parking cuts bays from a rotated grid inside the boundary. All of it is our own code from published geometry.
