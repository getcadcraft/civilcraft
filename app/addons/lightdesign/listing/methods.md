Direct light is the inverse-square cosine law on the luminaire's own candela table (.ies or .ldt). The room's surfaces are cut into patches, each a matt reflector with the reflectance you give; the light that bounces between them is solved with a radiosity iteration (disc form factors, rows scaled to one, Gauss-Seidel). The workplane grid is spaced by the rule of EN 12464-1. Glare is the CIE 117 UGR formula as an estimate. All lux are maintained values (light loss factor, 0.8 by default).

Daylight uses the CIE standard overcast sky, whose luminance is Lz (1 + 2 sin g) / 3 at altitude g, so an unobstructed horizontal plane gets 7 pi / 9 times Lz. Each window is cut into small cells; a point receives T times the sky luminance times the two cosines times the cell area over the distance squared from each cell; directions below the horizon give nothing. The light that comes in is added to the patches and bounced like electric light. Furniture and partitions are boxes; a luminaire reaches a point only if the straight line crosses no box (a slab test); bounced light is not blocked. Emergency lighting is the direct floor illuminance from the luminaires on the emergency circuit at their emergency output, along the route's centre line and over the open area, reflections ignored. Road lighting follows the conventions described in K. Sørensen, Road lighting (NMF textbook, 2013) and Fotios, Boyce and Ellis (UK DfT, 2005): illuminance I cos v / d², luminance 0.0001 r I / h² from the r-table you give (linear in β and tan γ), an observer in the middle of each lane 60 m before the field between two luminaires, eyes 1.5 m up, the lowest average, Uo and Ul over the observers, veiling luminance K Σ E / θ² with K = 10 (or from age) and TI = 65 Lv / L^0.8 from initial values, luminaires up to 500 m ahead and below a 20 degree roof line, and the edge illumination ratio on strips one lane wide.
