A typical year is what the weather is usually like. It is not a forecast, not the worst case, and not a record of any real year. Design figures size equipment for most hours, not all of them.

The nearest station is not your site. Hills, the coast, a valley floor or a city centre can differ from an airport 20 km away. Check the figures against local knowledge, and use your authority's own design values where it publishes them.

A compliance calculation may require its own weather file and its own software. In California, for example, the energy code's performance method uses the weather files the Energy Commission names, in software it has approved. This add-on does not replace that.

The first weather set covers California only. Other places need a weather file of your own until more sets are added.

The year viewer shows what the weather file holds. An hour the file marks as missing is left as a gap, never filled in. Some files carry no data in some columns (rain and snow are often empty in older typical years); those quantities are not offered. The wet-bulb temperature, humidity ratio, enthalpy and height of the sun are worked out from the file, not measured. Sunrise, sunset and the height of the sun are good to about a minute of time, in the station's standard time. The comfort range is a plain range of air temperature and relative humidity that you set: it is not a comfort standard and leaves out radiant temperature, air speed, clothing and activity. Changing the file multiplies or shifts one quantity for the whole year; it does not edit single hours, humidity does not follow a temperature change, and the design conditions in the file's header are not worked out again, so a changed file is for trying things out, not for sizing or compliance.
