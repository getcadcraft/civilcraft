It is a calculation aid, not an approval: the numbers are not a certificate and a water engineer decides what they are worth. It has been tested on made-up networks against hand answers, not yet on a real utility's model, so compare it with your own before you rely on it. It does not yet compare scenarios, simulate water hammer, calibrate a model to field readings, size pipes or pumps for you, model leakage or pressure-dependent demand, read SCADA, or reconcile with GIS. Chlorine decay is first order only, a tank is one mixed volume, booster stations are not modelled, a pipe cut partly by a valve is closed whole in an outage, and the day patterns and demand table are starting values. It does not design sewers; that is a separate planned add-on.
