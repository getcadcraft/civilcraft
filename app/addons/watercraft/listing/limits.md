It is a calculation aid, not an approval: the numbers are not a certificate and a water engineer decides what they are worth. It has been tested on made-up networks against hand answers, not yet on a real utility's model, so compare it with your own before you rely on it. It does not yet simulate water hammer, read live SCADA data, size pumps, or reconcile with GIS. Chlorine decay is first order only, a tank is one mixed volume, a pipe cut partly by a valve is closed whole in an outage, and the day patterns and demand table are starting values. Two throttled valves in series are settled one after the other (an approximation), pressure rules are checked at step boundaries only, pump efficiency is one number a pump, and the scenarios, rules and valves you type are not saved in the project file yet. Pipe sizing is a greedy search, not a guaranteed cheapest design, and the prices are yours: none ship with the add-on. It does not design sewers; that is a separate planned add-on.
